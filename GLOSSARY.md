# Glossary

Terms the kitchen app uses, so code, cards and tests say the same thing. Decisions live in the Notion Decisions database, not here.

- **Household**: the group of people who share one kitchen: its recipes, pantry, shopping lists and money. There is one household for now (ADR-25), and every API request acts within it.
- **Household member**: someone allowed into a household, identified by their Microsoft Entra object ID (`oid`). Today the only member is Adam. A signed-in account that isn't a member is refused (403).
- **Product**: something the household buys or keeps, tied to one food (several products can share a food, such as two brands of flour). It holds current stock in a unit and the latest price with its date.
- **Staple**: a product flagged as tracked. Only staples are tracked; a food with no staple product, or whose staples have run out, counts as "assume we need it" when planning a shop.
- **Pantry snapshot**: the one row per product per day (UTC) holding its stock and price that day, written by a daily timer. Running again the same day overwrites that day's row; a missed day can't be rebuilt (ADR-28).
- **Receipt**: one store receipt the household saved: store, date and total in whole cents. It is added only through the MCP's `add_receipt` (card P4), after the person confirms the lines Claude read. Its text is untrusted data. The same store, date and total is refused unless the person says it was a second purchase.
- **Price ledger**: one row for every line of every receipt (`price_ledger`), whether or not it matched: the line's own words, cents paid (below 0 for a discount), how many packages, a package's size, and the food and product where it matched. A line matches only on the exact name (ignoring case and extra spaces) of a product or food: the kitchen name the person confirmed when one is given, otherwise the receipt's wording. Nothing is guessed, no foods or products are made, and unmatched lines are reported. A matched staple takes the line's package price (a discount on its own line isn't taken off), dated the receipt's date, only when that is newer than its price date.
- **Receipt reminder**: one phone push on Sunday evening (US Central), through the same ntfy topic as the bank sync's alerts, asking the person to log receipts (card P5). It goes out only for a week (Monday to Sunday, ending that evening) that has a planned meal, and once per week: `receipt_reminder` records the weeks sent, and a push ntfy refuses isn't recorded.
- **Cooked event**: one row each time someone presses "I made this" on a recipe (or asks Claude to, the MCP's `mark_cooked`): the household, recipe (name copied in), servings made and when. Pressing it also uses up pantry stock: each ingredient, scaled to servings made, comes off the matching products. Foods with no product are ignored and stock stops at 0; what ran short is reported as a shortfall.
- **Meal plan**: the household's planned meals. The week view shows seven days (Monday to Sunday), today's meals first.
- **Planned meal**: one recipe set for a date and a meal slot, with a servings count. It starts at the recipe's own servings (1 if the recipe has none). The same recipe may be planned twice in a slot. Deleting a recipe removes its planned meals.
- **Meal slot**: which meal of the day a planned meal is for: breakfast, lunch, dinner or snack, shown in that order.
- **Shopping list**: what to buy for a stretch of days (by default the coming week: today and the six days after). Built from the meal plan: each food is totalled over the planned recipes (scaled by planned servings ÷ recipe servings, converted between units of one kind), then stock held in staples is taken off. A food with no staple, or whose staple ran out, stays on ("assume we need it"). Amounts of one food in different kinds of unit (grams and a count) are separate lines. Making a new list keeps the old ones, and brings the previous list's unticked hand-added items along.
- **Shopping item**: one line on a shopping list, either worked out from the plan (`plan`) or typed in by hand (`manual`), with a ticked-off flag for use in the store.
- **Recipe draft**: a recipe read from a website's schema.org JSON-LD (`POST /api/recipe-import`, card K2), shown in the recipe editor to check. In the app nothing is saved until the person saves it; the MCP's `save_recipe` saves the draft straight away. Ingredient lines are matched to the household's foods and units where the wording clearly matches; the rest become new foods.
- **Import link**: a row in `import_source` remembering which kitchen row an item imported from another system (Mealie, card K3; Grocy products, card K5) became, plus a fingerprint of what was imported. A second import run uses it to skip unchanged items, update changed ones and never duplicate. Mealie meal plan entries become planned meals; entries that are only a note are skipped and reported. Grocy products become pantry products: stock and the latest price come from Grocy, and a Grocy minimum stock above 0 makes a staple.
- **Account**: a bank account or card the household's transactions come from (`money_account`). It keeps Plaid's id for it and the last digits the bank shows (the mask), never the full number.
- **Transaction**: one movement of money on an account: a date, an amount in whole cents, a payee, the bank's description text and, when it came from Plaid, Plaid's transaction id (unique per household, so importing it again updates it instead of adding another). Money out (spending) is negative and money in is positive; Plaid's own amounts are the other way round and the import flips them. Only posted transactions are kept; pending ones are skipped because Plaid gives them a new id when they post. Transactions come only from the bank import: nothing in the app or the MCP creates one or changes its amount or account (ADR-27).
- **Category**: what money went on, such as Groceries; names are unique per household. A transaction has one category, or is split, or is uncategorized. Its category was set either by a rule or by hand (`manual`); rules never change one set by hand. Each category has a kind (card $6): spending (the default) or not spending, for transfers, pay and other income.
- **Split**: one transaction divided across two or more categories. The parts are whole cents with the transaction's sign, each in a different category, and always add up exactly to the transaction (checked before saving). A split transaction has no single category. If the bank later changes the amount, the parts no longer add up, so the import clears them and the transaction is uncategorized again.
- **Rule**: sets a category automatically when its text matches a transaction's payee or description, either the whole text (`equals`) or anywhere in it (`contains`), ignoring case and extra spaces. Rules run in their order and the first match wins. They run on every import and on "Run rules now", and only touch transactions not categorized by hand and not split; a category a rule set is cleared when no rule matches any more.
- **Budget**: a spending category's monthly amount (`money_budget`); a not-spending category can't have one, and one set before it became not spending is hidden until it is spending again. It is set from a month onwards and carries to later months without being entered again, until a later month changes or clears it. There is no envelope rollover: what's left of one month doesn't move to the next (ADR-27).
- **Spend** (in a month, per spending category): money out in that category during the month, counted on posted dates, less refunds (money in under the same category). A transaction counts in its own category; a split counts each part in its part's category. Uncategorized money counts nowhere, and not-spending categories are left out entirely, so a transfer or a paycheck is never spending or a refund, even when it's categorized. A spending category with no budget is listed only when money went out in it. Spend vs budget lists Groceries first.
- **Bank connection**: one bank login connected through Plaid Link (Plaid calls it an item), with the accounts it gives (`plaid_item`, `plaid_item_account`). Plaid's access token for it lives only in Key Vault; the database keeps the secret's name. Connecting a new one in Production uses up one of the Plaid Trial's 10 connections for good, so the app asks first. When the bank needs the login repaired, the connection shows Reconnect, which runs Link's update mode on the same login, so transaction ids don't change.
- **Bank sync**: the daily run (and "Sync now") that fetches every change since each connection's cursor from Plaid's `/transactions/sync` and saves it through the Plaid import (see Transaction). The cursor moves only once that connection's rows are saved, so a failed run is retried in full next time. Each run's result is kept on the connection, and any failure sends one phone alert through ntfy.
- **Withdrawn transaction**: a saved transaction the bank later removed (Plaid's `removed`). It is kept, never deleted, and listed on the Banks screen for a person to check (`plaid_withdrawn`). Removed pending transactions were never saved, so they aren't reported.
- **Token vault**: the second Key Vault in each environment, holding only the Plaid access tokens the API writes when a bank is connected. The API may write there and only read the main vault, so it can never change who is a household member.
